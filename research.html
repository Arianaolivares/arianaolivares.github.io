M.S. thesis, in progress

Injection molding of soft robotic components using a pellet extrusion head

Nemitz Robotics Group, Tufts University. Advisor: Prof. Markus Nemitz.

This thesis evaluates injection molding with a pellet extrusion head as a low-cost method for fabricating simple soft robotic components. The process targets ultra-soft elastomers that are difficult to 3D print directly, with part production times on the order of minutes. The system currently molds benchmark geometries, including cubes and tensile dog-bone specimens, and is being extended to more complex parts and additional materials. Molded parts are benchmarked against those from a commercial injection molding machine.

Planned work includes an interchangeable PLA extrusion head that would allow the same system to print its own molds, as well as image-based characterization of molded parts. The project supports the group’s broader goal of low-cost landmine-detecting robots that can be produced with widely available 3D printers.

Undergraduate research

Pellet printing for soft devices

Nemitz Robotics Group, Tufts University. With Yijia Wu and Prof. Markus P. Nemitz.

Most soft robots are made either by casting soft silicones or by filament 3D printing, which is limited to much stiffer materials. This project explored fused granulate fabrication (FGF), or pellet-based 3D printing, as a low-cost alternative that is as simple as filament printing but works with materials nearly as soft as cast silicone. Using the group’s pellet printer, I evaluated printer performance through mechanical testing and demonstrations.

For the group’s pellet printing paper, I was responsible for the comparison between 3D-printed and cast PneuNet actuators, including fabricating the cast benchmark actuators and setting up tensile and inflation-cycle testing. The printed PneuNets kept their structural integrity over 100,000 inflation cycles, and the team printed a soft robotic fish with integrated pneumatic actuators that required no post-assembly.

Freestanding magneto-mechanical metamaterials

Zhao Lab, Stanford University. With Jay Sim, Sarah Hwang, and Prof. Renee Zhao.

Studied freestanding magneto-mechanical metamaterials with out-of-plane magnetization. I fabricated silicone and NdFeB unit cells in 3D-printed molds, magnetized them at controlled angles using custom fixtures in a 1.5 T impulse field, and assembled them into arrays that rise into 3D shapes under a magnetic field. Measured shapes, extracted with a MATLAB image-processing pipeline, were compared against Abaqus simulations, which captured the dominant folding behavior.

The project was conducted in collaboration with Prof. Wei Chen’s group at Northwestern University, toward design-optimized metamaterials that morph from flat sheets into complex 3D geometries.

Summer 2024
Stanford SURF

Design and actuation of conformal magneto-mechanical metamaterials

Zhao Lab, Stanford University. With Jay Sim, Sarah Hwang, and Prof. Renee Zhao.

Designed unit cells that allow magneto-mechanical metamaterial arrays to conform to cylindrical, concave, and convex substrates, then fabricated, magnetized, and assembled the arrays and actuated them between paired electromagnetic coils. I also tested a hollow array wrapped around a discrete iron-core coil, which reached peak actuation at 22.5 A. Experimental deformation closely matched Abaqus simulations, and the results showed how magnetic particle concentration and applied current shape the actuation.
