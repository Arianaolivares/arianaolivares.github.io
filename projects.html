Senior capstone

Secure after-hours donation system

With Sol Brizuela Baez and Nathan Henderson, for Goodwill Industries of West Michigan

Featured in Tufts Now

Donations left outside Goodwill locations after hours are exposed to theft, weather, and breakage. The team developed a two-door interlock system in which an interior, stepper-driven door opens only after the donor-facing door is closed, and a level conveyor transfers items into a locked enclosure without a vertical drop. A PIR sensor, limit switches, and ultrasonic sensors disable the interior door whenever a person is present. Finite element analysis was used to evaluate the three-point locking configuration under prying loads.

During a two-hour public demonstration, the system completed repeated donation cycles without jamming, and at least 90% of approximately 30 participants completed a donation using only the posted instructions. Goodwill has shared the design with its safety team for possible future use.

Undergraduate assistive design

Wheelchair tire cleaning station

Team of four, designed for a client who uses a powered wheelchair

A drive-on platform that allows the client to clean his wheelchair tires at his entryway without hand use or caregiver assistance. The team adapted a heavy-duty motorcycle wheel stand, integrated replaceable coarse-bristle brush modules, and designed a custom one-way roller consisting of a lathe-turned aluminum shaft, a machined keyway, and a press-fit one-way bearing. The base was waterjet-cut from aluminum, the complete device weighs under 10 lb, and in testing with mud it cleaned the tires in approximately two minutes.
