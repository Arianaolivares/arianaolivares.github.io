About

I am an M.S. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Tufts University, advised by Prof. Markus Nemitz in the Nemitz Robotics Group. My research focuses on advanced manufacturing for soft robotics, particularly fabrication methods that reduce the cost and technical barriers of producing soft robotic components.

My thesis evaluates injection molding with a pellet extrusion head as a route to fabricating ultra-soft elastomer components, contributing to the lab’s effort to make soft robots that can be built with widely available 3D printing hardware. Previously, I completed two Stanford Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowships in the Zhao Lab, where I studied magnetically actuated soft metamaterials.

I also serve as a teaching assistant in robotics at Tufts and am committed to broadening access to engineering education. My CV is available here.

Research

Current
M.S. thesis

Injection molding of soft robotic components using a pellet extrusion head

Nemitz Robotics Group, Tufts University

This thesis evaluates injection molding with a pellet extrusion head as a low-cost method for fabricating simple soft robotic components. The process targets ultra-soft elastomers that are difficult to 3D print directly, with part production times on the order of minutes. The system currently molds benchmark geometries, including cubes and tensile dog-bone specimens, and is being extended to more complex parts and additional materials. Molded parts are benchmarked against those from a commercial injection molding machine.

Planned work includes an interchangeable PLA extrusion head that would allow the same system to print its own molds, as well as image-based characterization of molded parts. The project supports the group’s broader goal of low-cost landmine-detecting robots that can be produced with widely available 3D printers.

Pellet-based 3D printing of ultra-soft actuators

Nemitz Robotics Group, Tufts University

Soft magnetic metamaterials with programmable deformation

Zhao Lab, Stanford University (SURF)

Investigated soft magnetic metamaterials capable of programmable deformation under external magnetic fields. Fabricated and characterized silicone–magnetic composite unit cells, analyzed their mechanical response, and modeled actuation behavior to inform the design of untethered soft robots.

Projects

Spring 2026
Client project

Wheelchair tire cleaning station

Team of four, designed for a client who uses a powered wheelchair

A drive-on platform that allows the client to clean his wheelchair tires at his entryway without hand use or caregiver assistance. The team adapted a heavy-duty motorcycle wheel stand, integrated replaceable coarse-bristle brush modules, and designed a custom one-way roller consisting of a lathe-turned aluminum shaft, a machined keyway, and a press-fit one-way bearing. The base was waterjet-cut from aluminum, and the complete device weighs under 10 lb.

In testing with mud, the station cleaned the tires in approximately two minutes.

Fall 2025
Senior capstone

Secure after-hours donation system

Team of three, for Goodwill Industries of Greater Michigan

Donations left outside Goodwill locations after hours are exposed to theft, weather, and breakage. The team developed a two-door interlock system in which an interior, stepper-driven door opens only after the donor-facing door is closed, and a level conveyor transfers items into a locked enclosure without a vertical drop. A PIR sensor, limit switches, and ultrasonic sensors disable the interior door whenever a person is present. Finite element analysis was used to evaluate the three-point locking configuration under prying loads.

During a two-hour public demonstration, the system completed repeated donation cycles without jamming, and at least 90% of approximately 30 participants completed a donation using only the posted instructions.
